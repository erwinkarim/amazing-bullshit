### The Echo at Tell es-Sultan

**Setting:** Jericho, c. 8,500 BCE. At the edge of the Jordan Valley sits Tell es-Sultan. Rising 28 feet above a ring of mud-brick homes stands the Tower of Jericho—humanity’s very first monumental watchtower.

---

### Act I: "There Must Be Some Way Out of Here"

The night wind off the Judean desert smelled of parched earth and old wheat chaff. In the shadow of Jericho’s newly raised stone wall, two men huddled beside a dying hearth.

The older one, an itinerant storyteller whom the village elders called a fool, spat into the dust. *"There must be some way out of here,"* he muttered, rubbing wrists chafed from hauling heavy granite quern-stones.

Beside him sat a young hunter whose eyes darted nervously to the shadows—a thief by the settlement's new laws, caught taking a gazelle from the Headman’s private reserve.

*"There’s too much confusion,"* the thief whispered. *"When my grandfathers followed the herds, the sky belonged to everyone. Now they build walls, press marks into clay, and claim the spring belongs to three families. I can't get no relief."*

The storyteller looked toward the lit compound of the Granary Master. *"The merchants drink my palm wine. The plowmen break their spines digging my earth for grain they'll never be allowed to taste. None of them along the line know what any of it is worth. They think civilization means hoarding more than your neighbor can steal."*

---

### Act II: "Life Is But a Joke"

The thief reached for his obsidian knife. *"We could burn the granaries tonight. Scatter the herd. Force them back to the open plain."*

*"No reason to get excited,"* the storyteller spoke softly, resting a hand on the young man's arm. *"There are many here among us who feel that life is but a joke—a trick played by priests who claim the gods gave them the water. But you and I, we’ve seen the world outside these walls. We know this isn't our fate, and the hour is getting late."*

Above them, looming in the torchlight, stood the stone watchtower. Atop its flat summit, the chieftains and their armed sentries—the world's first "princes"—kept their view. They looked down over the enclosed oasis, convinced that their stone walls, stored grain, and guards made them invincible.

Below the tower, women walked in silence, balancing heavy clay jars from the spring. Barefoot servants swept the threshing floors, tired and nameless, laboring for an empire they would never own.

---

### Act III: The Wind Begins to Howl

The storyteller stood up and pointed out toward the black expanse of the valley, far beyond the reach of Jericho’s fires.

*"Listen,"* he said.

Outside in the cold distance, a wildcat growled. A sudden, violent gust of wind whipped down from the limestone cliffs, blowing dust into the eyes of the sentries atop the tower. The torches flickered and died out.

Through the howling gale came the rhythmic clatter of hooves on river stone. Two riders were approaching through the gloom—outsiders, storm-bringers, the inevitable cycle of upheaval that no wall could ever keep out.

The chieftains on the watchtower gripped their stone spears, suddenly looking very small against the dark.
