## The 30-Second Polaroid

Arthur bought the vintage Polaroid camera from a dusty estate sale for fifteen bucks. The seller—a twitchy old woman who smelled faintly of copper—insisted it was "already loaded" and refused to take a penny more.

When Arthur got back to his apartment, he aimed it at his kitchen island and pressed the shutter. The camera whirred, spitting out a blank square of film. As the colors slowly bloomed, Arthur frowned. In the photo, a red ceramic mug sat shattered on the tile floor.

"That's weird," he muttered, picking up his coffee mug from the counter.

Precisely thirty seconds later, his elbow twitched, sending the mug crashing down. It shattered into the **exact same pattern** as the photograph.

His heart did a strange little flip.

To test it, Arthur walked to the living room window and pointed the lens at the empty rain-slicked street below. *Click.* He waited. The photo developed to show a yellow taxi pulled up to the curb, a woman in a bright blue raincoat stepping out.

He checked his watch. Twenty-eight seconds later, a yellow cab screeched to a halt below. A woman in a blue raincoat popped her umbrella open and stepped into the storm.

The camera wasn't taking pictures of the present. **It was showing him thirty seconds into the future.**

Adrenaline hummed in his veins. The possibilities were endless—lottery tickets, stock trades, casino tables. He grinned, feeling like he’d just stumbled onto the cheat code to the universe.

Just to see his own face in the future, he turned the camera around, held it out at arm's length, and pointed it at himself sitting on the sofa.

*Click.*

---

The film ejected with a soft hiss. Arthur leaned back, shaking the print, watching the dark shapes slowly resolve into light.

First, his own face appeared. But he wasn't grinning.

In the photo, his eyes were stretched wide with sheer, unadulterated terror. His mouth was open in a silent scream, and his hand was pointing frantically toward the lens.

Arthur’s laughter died in his throat.

As the background of the photo settled, his blood turned to ice. Standing in the darkened hallway directly behind his sofa was a towering, malformed silhouette—a pale, faceless thing reaching a long, needle-fingered hand toward the back of his neck.

Arthur froze.

He looked down at his watch.

*Ten seconds...*

He stared frantically at the empty hallway ahead of him. Nothing was there. Just the quiet shadows of his locked apartment.

*Five seconds...*

He tried to stand, but his legs felt like lead.

*Three seconds...*

Then, right against the bare skin on the back of his neck, Arthur felt the ice-cold prickle of long, thin fingers... and heard a soft, wet breath behind his ear.
