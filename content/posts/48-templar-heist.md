The midday sun hung over the Levant like an anvil, baking the cracked earth into fine white dust. Through the shimmer of the heat haze, a heavily guarded convoy rolled along a deserted trade road, bound for the coastal fortress at Acre. Iron-rimmed wheels groaned beneath the weight of three sturdy carriages, each packed with iron-bound chests of gold bullion being transferred from an inland Templar vault. Surrounding the cargo was a squad of twelve armored knights, their white surcoats bearing the crimson cross, swords glinting in the blinding glare.

Without warning, the lead knight reined in his warhorse.

Standing squarely in the center of the road, fifty paces ahead, was a solitary man. He wore strange, tightly fitted clothing the color of desert sand, completely devoid of chainmail, plate, or heraldry. He stood perfectly still, watching the oncoming force with unbothered calm.

"Out of the way, fool!" shouted the Templar commander, his hand dropping to the pommel of his broadsword. "You stand in the path of the Holy Order. Step aside or be trampled!"

The man didn't flinch. He raised his voice, clear and steady across the silent plain. "I need the gold you’re carrying. Put it on the road and I’ll be on my way."

For a moment, the only sound was the dry wind rustling through the scrubland. Then, the knights erupted into booming laughter. The commander shook his head, drawing his heavy broadsword with a sharp rasp of steel. "You dare threaten the warriors of Christ alone? Kill him!"

Four knights spurred their heavy warhorses into a full gallop, iron-clad hooves pounding the earth like thunder as they leveled their lances.

The stranger moved with fluid, terrifying speed. His hands vanished beneath his jacket and emerged holding two black, square-framed **Glock 17** pistols.

Before the knights could even process the strange black objects, thunder cracked across the desert. A rapid rhythm of deafening gunshots shattered the medieval silence. Nine-millimeter rounds tore through leather, chainmail, and helmet with brutal precision. The four charging riders tumbled from their saddles, dead before their bodies hit the dirt. Their panic-stricken horses veered off into the brush.

As the remaining Templars froze in shock, their mounts rearing in terror at the unnatural roar, the man holstered both handguns in a single swift motion. In the same breath, he unslung an **M4 carbine** from across his back, snapped the stock into his shoulder, and flipped the selector switch.

Muzzle flash burst from the barrel like a dragon's breath. A hail of high-velocity rifle rounds ripped through the air, punching effortlessly through iron plate armor and wooden shields alike. Screams of confusion were instantly drowned out by the roar of automatic fire and the metallic clatter of spent brass casings raining onto the sand. In less than ten seconds, the entire Templar escort lay motionless in the dust.

Silence settled over the road, thick and absolute.

The man lowered his rifle, the barrel smoking in the dry heat. With practiced, methodical efficiency, he stepped over the fallen knights and approached the carriages. One by one, he hauled the heavy, iron-strapped chests of Templar gold out of the wagons, dragging them into a tight, consolidated pile in the middle of the road.

Stepping up to the gold, he unclipped a black walkie-talkie from his chest rig and clicked the transmitter.

"Package secured," he said quietly into the mic. "Requesting immediate retrieval."

A sharp squelch of static answered him. A fraction of a second later, a brilliant, hum-filled spectre of blinding blue light enveloped the man and the pile of gold. A violent rush of displaced air swept the dirt track—and when the glare vanished, the road was empty. Only the abandoned carriages and the quiet dust remained.
