The Final Decision

techjourneyman included in What-If
 2026-08-13  640 words   4 minutes 

The secure video bridge from NATO headquarters in Brussels was a grid of pale, terrified faces. Eight heads of state sat in isolated rooms across the continent, lit only by the harsh glow of emergency monitors.

“We have six minutes,” the briefing officer in Brussels stammered, his voice straining against rising panic. “The launch was detected twelve minutes ago in the mid-Atlantic abyssal plain. Multiple submarine-launched ballistic missiles. Flight trajectories confirm high-altitude re-entry targeting major hubs—London, Berlin, Warsaw, Paris.”

“Where are they coming from?!” the German Chancellor demanded, his hands gripping the edges of his desk until his knuckles turned white. “What does Washington say? We need attribution!”

“We had US Cyber Command and the Joint Chiefs on the line three minutes ago,” the officer replied, wiping sweat from his forehead. “NORAD confirms the launches, but the radar signatures are completely scrubbed. No transponders, no thermal signatures trailing back to a known naval vessel. It could be Russia. It could be a rogue diesel-electric sub. It could be a false-flag spoof. Washington refuses to execute a retaliatory strike until attribution is absolute.”

“Absolute?!” the Polish Prime Minister snapped, leaning into his camera. “In six minutes, Warsaw is a crater! By the time they attribute the launch, Europe is ash!”

The digital clock anchored at the top of the shared screen ticked down relentlessly: 05:12.

In the silence that followed, every gaze on the conference call slowly turned toward the top-left tile of the video screen.

Palais de l’Élysée.

With the United Kingdom’s nuclear deterrent functionally tied into NATO’s joint command structure, France possessed Europe’s only fully independent, sovereign second-strike capability. The Force de frappe. Somewhere deep in the North Atlantic, a French Triomphant-class nuclear submarine was running silent, its sixteen M51 ballistic missiles waiting for a single encrypted command to turn an entire hemisphere into glass.

President Laurent sat motionless in Paris. Beside his desk lay the black leather briefcase—la mallette nucléaire. In his right hand, he held the golden authentication key.

“Mr. President,” the Secretary General whispered from Brussels, his voice hollow. “If you do not order a counter-strike now, our window to retaliate evaporates. If Le Téméraire does not launch before impact…”

Laurent stared at the grid of faces. Friends. Allies. Men and women who, in less than five minutes, would cease to exist.

The weight pressing down on his chest was terrifying—an ocean of moral responsibility crushing a single human heart. To launch meant retaliation. But aimed at whom?

If he ordered a strike on Moscow, and the missiles had actually come from an undetected rogue element or a third-party nation trying to provoke a global extinction event, he would be the man who murdered hundreds of millions of innocent people and ignited World War III by mistake.

If he held his hand, he would be the leader who stood by while Western civilization was erased, leaving France’s dead unavenged and its posture of deterrence a tragic joke in the history books.

03:05.

“Laurent, please,” the German Chancellor choked out, tears finally breaking past his composure. “Give us an answer. Do we just sit here and die?”

The French President closed his eyes. In the suffocating quiet of the Élysée Palace, he could hear the steady, rhythmic ticking of an antique clock on the mantelpiece, mocking the digital countdown on the screen. He squeezed the authentication key so hard the metal bit painfully into his palm.

He had spent a lifetime studying statecraft, strategy, and power. Yet no doctrine, no military manual, no philosophical framework had prepared him for the agony of an anonymous apocalypse.

He opened his eyes, adjusted his tie, and looked directly into the lens of the camera. His thumb hovered less than an inch above the terminal’s activation switch.

“May God forgive us,” Laurent said, his voice terrifyingly calm. “For whatever comes next.”
