The Swiss Surprise

techjourneyman included in Comedy
 2026-07-31  603 words   3 minutes 

Julian’s Thursday lunch at Le Chalet Central was supposed to be completely unremarkable: a plate of veal cutlet, a glass of Pinot Noir, and forty-five minutes of staring blankly at his phone.

Then he went to wash his hands.

Sitting on the pristine white marble sink, directly between the lavender hand soap and a stack of folded linen towels, was an unzipped leather duffel bag. Peeking out from the top were thick, immaculate bricks of purple paper.

Julian froze. He stepped closer and squinted.

They were 1,000-Swiss-Franc notes. Banded in neat, industrial stacks of a hundred bills each. There were dozens of them. A conservative estimate put the pile at roughly four million francs.

He looked left. He looked right. He checked under the stall doors. Empty. The bathroom smelled subtly of peppermint, luxury, and life-changing crime.

This is a trap, Julian thought. A social experiment. A mob hit waiting to happen.

Then his brain corrected itself: It’s four million francs.

Moving with the desperate, frantic speed of a man who had never broken a rule in his life, Julian unzipped his oversized canvas coat. He began shoveling the cold bricks of cash into his inner pockets, his briefcase, and his waistband. Within ninety seconds, he was three inches wider and four million francs richer.

He straightened his tie, smoothed his hair, and walked back out to the dining room.

His heart pounded against the thick wall of cash hugging his ribs. He tried to act natural. He sat down, took a bite of his lukewarm cutlet, and gave a smooth, totally non-suspicious nod to a woman two tables over. She stared back with deep concern—likely because Julian was sweating profusely and his coat was making a loud, plastic crunching sound every time he breathed.

“Check, please!” Julian squeaked, his voice an octave higher than usual.

He slapped a 50-franc note on the table, waved off the change, and stood up. He was ten steps from the exit. Nine. Eight.

I’m going to buy an island, he thought. A small one. Near Italy.

He pushed open the heavy oak door and stepped into the crisp Zurich air—right into the chest of a man in a high-visibility yellow vest. Behind him stood a flatbed truck equipped with a massive industrial vacuum tube.

“Ah, Guten Tag, sir!” said a hyper-polite man holding a digital clipboard. “Apologies for the brief delay. Swiss Federal Mint Asset Reclamation Division.”

Julian froze, his briefcase clutched to his chest like a shield. “I… I’m just leaving.”

“Wonderful!” the inspector beamed. “We are just conducting our bi-hourly municipal washroom sweep. As per Federal Cleanliness Ordinance 412, all uncollected currency left on public sinks automatically becomes municipal property after three minutes.”

Before Julian could even attempt a lie, two uniformed Swiss technicians stepped forward with handheld currency detectors.

BEEP-BEEP-BEEP-BEEP.

“Ah, marvelous! You are carrying the local surplus!” the inspector cheered.

With terrifying, clinical Swiss efficiency, the technicians unzipped Julian’s coat, popped open his briefcase, and gently extracted every single brick of cash. They stacked the notes onto a velvet tray, loaded them into the truck’s pneumatic tube, and sucked all four million francs away with a satisfying FOOP.

Julian stood on the pavement, his coat flapping open in the wind, utterly speechless.

The inspector handed him a neatly printed slip of thermal paper. “Here is your official receipt of confiscation, sir. And, of course, your invoice.”

Julian blinked, his voice barely a whisper. “My… invoice?”

“Ja. A fifty-franc administrative fee for carrying non-approved municipal weight, plus twenty francs for delaying the reclamation crew. You may pay by card. Have a delightful afternoon!”
