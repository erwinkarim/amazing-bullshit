The Golden Cage

techjourneyman included in Cautionary Tale
 2026-08-06  596 words   3 minutes 

The morning alarm in the Grand Palace of Aurelia was not a rude chime, but a live harpist performing softly from behind a silk screen.

Prince Kael opened his eyes beneath a grand canopy woven from pure silver thread. Within seconds of his eyelids fluttering, a choreographed army of attendants glided into the chamber. One offered a warm linen towel infused with rare orchid essence; another held a tray bearing an espresso dusted with edible 24-karat gold; a third knelt quietly, presenting a velvet tray of five custom diamond cufflinks.

To the outside world, Aurelia was the jewel of the continent—a paradise of unlimited wealth, automated luxury, and tax-free paradise. But as Kael sat up, the heavy silk sheets felt less like comfort and more like a shroud.

Today was the Day of the Gilded Summit, and the palace was in a state of controlled panic.

As Kael stepped into his dressing gallery, a master tailor rushed forward to fit a coat made of peacock silk and laced with crushed emeralds. The tailor’s hands trembled so violently that he accidentally pricked Kael’s thumb with a silver pin.

“Forgive me, Your Highness!” the tailor gasped, dropping to his knees, his face pale with sheer terror. “Please—the Minister of Image said if the attire is anything less than immaculate today, my family’s city visa will be revoked.”

Kael looked down at the tiny bead of red blood welling on his thumb. “It’s alright,” Kael whispered, reaching down to help the man up. “It was an accident.”

Before the tailor could stand, the grand doors burst open. King Vane stepped into the room, flanked by two armed guards in ceremonial platinum armor. The King’s smile was wide and radiant for the floating media drones hovering overhead, but his eyes were entirely dead.

“Stand straight, Kael,” the King commanded softly, his voice sharp enough to cut glass. He took Kael’s hand and unceremoniously wiped the blood away with a silk handkerchief, throwing it onto the floor.

Vane then adjusted the heavy, diamond-encrusted band wrapping tightly around Kael’s left wrist. To the public, it was the Royal Heirloom of Unity. In reality, it was a biometric inhibitor. If Kael’s heart rate spiked from anxiety, anger, or grief while in front of the cameras, the band delivered a subtle chemical tranquilizer to keep his posture relaxed and his smile serene.

“The foreign investors are already watching,” the King whispered, stepping dangerously close. “The city’s debt is mounting, the eastern power grid failed overnight, and the offshore accounts are frozen. If you look anything less than blissfully triumphant today, the market collapses by noon. Do you understand?”

“Yes, Father,” Kael murmured.

Kael walked out onto the high marble balcony overlooking the capital. Below him stretched a breathtaking vista of soaring glass skyscrapers, sky-bridges, and fountains spraying illuminated water into the morning air. Thousands of citizens gathered in the plaza below, waving golden flags and cheering his name.

He raised his hand and offered the world a flawless, radiant smile.

The crowds roared with adoration, blinded by the sun reflecting off the palace’s solid-gold spires. But from his high vantage point, Kael could see past the gleaming towers to the edge of the city, where massive concrete walls hid the sprawling, darkened outer districts that had been stripped of electricity just to keep the palace lights burning.

He took a sip of his gold-dusted espresso. It tasted metallic, cold, and bitter. He held his breath, willing his pulse to stay slow, trapped in a cage where even his own heart wasn’t allowed to bleed.
