Shadows Over the Channel

techjourneyman included in Alternate Reality
 2026-07-28  505 words   3 minutes 

Paris, May 2002.

Rain brushed against the tall arched windows of the restored Tuileries Palace, casting a soft sheen over the courtyard where imperial guards in tailored blue-and-gold uniforms stood at attention. Inside the Grand Cabinet, sleek flat-panel screens flickered discreetly beside gold-leafed furniture and silk tapestries—a subtle blend of 21st-century technology and classic imperial grandeur.

Emperor Napoleon VI stood with his hands folded behind his back, looking out over the gardens. At sixty-two, he possessed the sharp, hawkish gaze of his famous ancestor, though his empire was built on economic consolidation rather than endless conquest. Nearly two centuries earlier, Napoleon I had made the decisive choice: he halted his armies at the borders of Continental Europe, turned away from the treacherous frosts of Russia, and abandoned ambitions in Africa.

By keeping his empire bounded between the Atlantic and the Vistula, the Great Emperor had secured a Pax Napoleonica that survived into the modern era.

The heavy oak doors creaked open.

“Announcing His Excellency, Sir Reginald Vance, Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary from the United Kingdom,” the Master of Ceremonies announced.

Sir Reginald stepped into the hall, carrying a polished leather case containing his diplomatic credentials. He was a seasoned diplomat from London—a power that had remained a dominant maritime empire, forever watching the European titan across the water.

“Your Imperial Majesty,” Reginald said, offering a crisp, measured bow.

“Welcome to Paris, Sir Reginald,” Napoleon VI replied, stepping forward and gesturing toward two velvet armchairs near a crackling fireplace. “I trust your transit across the Channel was agreeable? I hear the high-speed rail line is proving quite efficient.”

“Seamless, Sire,” Reginald noted, taking a seat. “Though my 19th-century predecessors might turn in their graves knowing an Englishman can travel from London to Paris in under two hours without stepping onto a ship.”

Napoleon laughed softly, pouring two glasses of vintage Bordeaux.

“History is full of former impossibilities that become daily conveniences, Ambassador. My great-great-grandfather realized that Europe’s strength lay in continental harmony, not reckless overreach. He left the high seas to Britain and kept the mainland for the Grand Confederation. A sensible division of labor, wouldn’t you say?”

Reginald accepted the glass, studying the Emperor carefully. “A division that has kept a tense, if profitable, peace for two hundred years, Sire. Though Whitehall always watches the Confederation’s economic reach with… vigilant interest.”

“As we watch the British Commonwealth,” Napoleon countered smoothly, raising his glass. “We have no interest in the distant east or the sands of the south. A unified, prosperous Europe from Lisbon to Warsaw is more than enough to keep my ministers busy in the 21st century.”

The Emperor smiled, a spark of genuine warmth breaking through his diplomatic composure.

“To a new century of quiet borders, Lord Vance. May the Channel always remain wide enough for our distinct identities, but narrow enough for partnership.”

Reginald clinked his glass against the Emperor’s. “To peace on the Continent, Your Majesty.”
