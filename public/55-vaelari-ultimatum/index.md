# The Vaelari Ultimatum


The sky over Johannesburg changed forever on a crisp morning in October 1985. Without warning, a colossal, rusted metallic monolith materialized through the clouds, casting a sprawling, ominous shadow across the Highveld. It did not fire a shot, nor did it attempt to communicate. It simply hovered, motionless, a quiet behemoth in the spring sky.

For fourteen long days, the world held its breath in an agonizing slow burn.

In Washington and Moscow, the Cold War came to a staggering, frozen standstill. Nuclear launch keys were held in white-knuckled grips, yet for the first time in four decades, Soviet General Secretaries and American Presidents picked up the red hotline phones not to threaten each other, but to ask the same terrified question: *Is it yours?* Below the ship, life rapidly unraveled. Stock markets collapsed, churches were flooded around the clock, and emergency broadcast systems blared continuous test tones into the night. Scientists analyzed high-resolution telephoto images of the vessel's hull, only to discover a heatless, sound-absorbing alloy that defied every law of modern physics.

Then, on the fifteenth day, the silence broke.

A massive ventral hatch hissed open, lowering a ramp toward the parched earth below. Figures descended, and a collective shudder ran through the observers. The entities were bipedal, but their movements were sickeningly unnatural—jerky, fluid, and frame-rate deficient, like stop-motion animation forced into reality. Their limbs seemed slightly too long for their shimmering, crystalline suits, tilting at angles that violated human anatomy.

One of the creatures stepped forward, raising a palm-sized obsidian wedge toward the sky.

Instantly, the signal hit. Across the globe, heavy wooden-cased CRT televisions buzzed violently with static before their cathode-ray tubes flared to life. Analog radio frequencies screamed with feedback, and rabbit-ear antennas picked up a forced, universal broadcast. The live news feeds from Johannesburg vanished. In their place, a chilling, multi-tonal harmonic voice reverberated directly from television speakers in every living language:

> *"We are the Vaelari. We left this planet millions of years ago when an extinction-level asteroid forced our exodus. We are the rightful owners of this planet. Please leave the planet within one week, or we will destroy you all."*

---

In a dimly lit living room in Soweto, a family stood frozen around their glowing television, the screen flickering snowy static over their stunned faces while outside, sirens began to wail in the distance.

Across the planet, the ultimatum shattered the illusions of human power. In the underground command bunkers of the Pentagon and the Kremlin, generals and statesmen sat in petrified silence, staring at nuclear readiness boards that now felt entirely useless against an ancient, orbital predator. In Tokyo, London, and New York, panic erupted into the streets as crowds swarmed grocery stores and gas stations, burning through fuel in a desperate, directionless flight out of major cities. Meanwhile, at the foot of the alien vessel, cults formed overnight, kneeling in the dust to pray for salvation from gods who had returned only to evict them. A heavy, suffocating despair settled over Earth—the horrifying realization that humanity’s entire existence was reduced to a seven-day countdown on a world that was never truly theirs.
