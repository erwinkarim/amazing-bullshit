# Twenty Years in Four Minutes


The fluorescent light overhead hummed with a low, agonizing drone. Between them sat a polished mahogany table, split down the middle by a crisp stack of white legal paper.

Arthur stared at the black ink of his own signature, still wet on the line marked *Petitioner*.

Across from him, Elena sat with her hands clasped tightly in her lap. Her shoulders were tense, framed by the quiet, sterile formality of the lawyer’s office. Twenty years. How had two decades of living, breathing, shared existence boiled down to a man in a charcoal suit pointing a Parker pen at a dotted line?

Arthur’s mind drifted backward, seeking the precise moment the foundation had cracked.

He remembered the wild, reckless magic of their youth. The rainy October night in 2028 when they ran six blocks through downtown traffic, laughing as water soaked her canvas sneakers, ending up in a neon-lit diner sharing a single slice of lukewarm cherry pie. He remembered the smell of wet latex paint and plaster on his thumb the weekend they bought the tiny fixer-upper on Elm Street, sleeping on a bare mattress on the floor because the moving truck was delayed.

He remembered the quiet valleys, too. The suffocating stillness after the third failed IVF round, when the silence in the kitchen grew so heavy that even the hum of the refrigerator felt like an accusation. The subtle shift over the years—how she began leaving cold cups of chamomile tea on her nightstand untouched, how his knees stopped brushing hers under the duvet, how "I love you" slowly degraded into "Did you remember to pay the electric bill?"

They hadn't stopped loving each other with a sudden clash of anger. They had simply worn each other down, like water smoothing river stones until nothing sharp or distinct remained.

"Mrs. Vance?" the lawyer asked gently, sliding the document across the mahogany wood. "Whenever you're ready."

Elena looked up. Her eyes, rimmed with soft red, locked onto Arthur's. There was no hatred in her gaze—only a crushing, beautiful longing. It was the look of someone watching a ship pull away from the harbor into thick fog, knowing they would never see the passenger again.

She picked up the pen. Her fingers trembled, but she leaned forward and pressed the nib to the paper.

*E-l-e-n-a...*

*Chime.*

A soft, synthesized chime vibrated behind Arthur's temples, shattering the heavy silence of the room.

*"End simulation,"* a cool, melodic voice echoed from everywhere and nowhere at once. *"Thank you for using LifeSync Preview Services. Please allow three seconds for neural re-alignment."*

The lawyer dissolved into thin air. The mahogany table collapsed into a dark, sticky high-top bar table. The gray acoustic tile ceiling melted away into ambient amber lighting and exposed brick. The sterile quiet was instantly swallowed by the heavy swell of low-fi jazz, clinking ice cubes, and the hum of a crowded lounge.

Arthur gasped, a sudden wave of vertigo washing over him. On the left side of his head, two small, silicone neural pulse pads detached with a faint mechanical hiss.

His right hand twitched, instinctively reaching to rub a wedding ring that wasn't there. His fingers felt smooth—no subtle calluses from twenty years of home repairs, no phantom weight of silver.

Across from him, Elena jolted in her seat, clutching the edge of the wooden table as if breaking through the surface of icy water. She blinked rapidly, her chest heaving under a sleek leather jacket—not the beige cardigan she had been wearing a second ago.

She reached up, stunned, as a single, genuine tear slipped down her cheek. Her face was younger, unlined by two decades of shared stress and sleepless nights.

They were sitting in a back booth at *The Catalyst*, a high-end downtown lounge.

It was eight o'clock on a Tuesday. It was their very first date.

Only four minutes had passed since they had tapped their smart-cards and agreed to try the venue's newest icebreaker: the *LifeSync 20-Year Compatibility Immersion*. A rapid-induction neural dream designed to simulate two full decades of potential romance, marriage, quiet decay, and compromise—all compressed into three hundred real-time seconds.

The atmosphere between them was electric, heavy, and intensely strange. They were technical strangers who had just spent a lifetime breathing the same air, sharing the same grief, and breaking each other's hearts.

Elena swallowed hard, wiping the tear from her cheek with the back of her thumb. She stared at him, wide-eyed, struggling to reconcile the man sitting in front of her with the husband she had just divorced in her mind.

"That..." Her voice cracked slightly. "That was... intense."

Arthur looked at her—really looked at her. He felt the residual phantom ache of twenty years of memories lingering in his chest, existing side-by-side with the thrill of holding a glass of bourbon on a night that had barely started.

A quiet, wonderfully wistful smile touched his lips. He let out a slow breath, releasing the phantom weight of the lawyer's office, and leaned back against the plush leather booth.

He lifted his glass toward her.

"I'm sorry it didn't work out in the end," Arthur said, his voice soft, steady, and entirely devoid of bitterness. "But for what it's worth... that was the most successful relationship I've ever had."

Elena stared at him for a beat. Then, the tension in her shoulders collapsed, and a rich, breathless laugh escaped her lips.

She reached across the tiny table—her real fingers brushing his real wrist—and picked up her drink.

"To twenty years," she whispered, her eyes shining under the warm amber lights. "So... do you want to skip dinner and go find some lukewarm cherry pie?"
