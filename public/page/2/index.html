Five years. Five years since she died, and five years since he’d decided that dying didn’t have to be permanent, not if you were clever enough, not if you loved someone enough. He’d built a machine in his garage that folded time the way some men folded paper cranes. Attempt one hundred and seventeen had failed last week. She’d been crossing the street when it happened — except this time it wasn’t the car he’d stopped. It was a bicycle he never saw coming.

Krater Field, Vienna -- 1943

techjourneyman published on 2026-08-10 included in What-If

Vienna, the edge of the Krater Feld, 15 January 1943

My people—Germans and Magyars, Czechs and Poles, Croats and Slovenes, Ruthenians and Italians, Jews and Romani, all who call this Empire home—

We gather today where a city once stood.

Thirty years ago, on a January morning much like this one, the heart of Vienna vanished. In an instant of light no astronomer has explained and no priest has fully consoled, two million souls were taken from us—among them my grandfather, the Emperor Franz Joseph, who had reigned since before living memory; my cousin Franz Ferdinand, who would have worn this crown before me; and countless ordinary Viennese—bakers, teachers, students, mothers—who simply woke that morning in the wrong part of a city they loved.
