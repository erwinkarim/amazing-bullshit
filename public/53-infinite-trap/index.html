The Infinite Trap

techjourneyman included in Time Travel
 2026-10-10  959 words   5 minutes 

For thirty-four years, Dr. Julian Ross lived in the gray glow of vacuum tubes, particle counters, and laser arrays. His colleagues dismissed him as a ghost haunting the sub-basement of the university physics building, but Julian knew the truth: spatial displacement was possible.

And on a cold rainy Tuesday at 3:14 AM, he proved it.

The primary test was simple: send a silver pocket watch across a ten-meter void between two containment rings. Julian initiated the sequence, the air hummed with ozone, and the watch vanished. But it didn’t appear on the receiving pad ten meters away. Instead, it clattered onto his desk—right next to a mug of coffee he had finished two hours earlier.

Julian stared at the watch. The second hand was ticking in synch with his wall clock, but the date display read yesterday.

He hadn’t just mastered teleportation. He had cracked time itself.

The nobility of pure scientific discovery dissolved almost instantly, replaced by a lifelong resentment of unearned poverty. Decades of grant rejections, ignored papers, and missed life events demanded compensation.

Julian refined the device, shrinking the massive ring array into a heavy, waist-mounted belt fed by a compact cold-fusion cell. He mapped out the temporal mechanics, setting quantum parameters for precise leaps.

His first voyage was modest: jump back twenty-four hours with the winning numbers for a massive state lottery. When he stepped through the blinding flash of white light, he found himself standing in his apartment, holding a winning ticket purchased hours prior.

Next came the stock market. Julian jumped backward to buy short-dated call options on obscure biotech firms hours before unexpected buyout announcements. Then came sports betting—parlaying improbable long-shots in championship games.

Within six months, Julian Ross was no longer a disgraced academic. He was a multi-billionaire titan of industry, operating out of a fortified glass penthouse atop a mega-skyscraper in New York, surrounded by private security and unfettered influence.

He had achieved everything he ever wanted. Power. Wealth. Invincibility.

Until the sirens started.

It began on a Thursday evening. Heavy automatic gunfire echoed through the reinforced titanium doors of his penthouse lobby. His private security detail was eliminated in seconds by a rival syndicate—a shadowy cartel Julian had unwittingly ruined during his aggressive takeover of a global defense conglomerate.

Simultaneously, his monitors flashed red with federal seizure warrants. In his ruthless march to the top, he had drawn the wrath of three government intelligence agencies, all convinced he was an international espionage operative using alien tech to disrupt global financial markets.

Tear gas cannisters shattered the floor-to-ceiling glass. Armed men in tactical gear kicked open the inner doors.

“Julian Ross! Put your hands on your head!”

Julian smirked. “Fools,” he muttered.

He keyed in the coordinates on his waist belt. He would simply jump back two years, avoid buying out the defense conglomerate, place his capital into safer real estate ventures, and live out his days in peace.

He tapped the activation sequence. The world dissolved into a blinding sea of white light.

The air cleared.

Julian was standing in a sprawling luxury estate overlooking a sunlit Mediterranean coastline. The smell of tear gas was gone. The gunshots had ceased.

He smiled, exhaling a breath of relief. He checked his wrist console to verify the temporal arrival coordinates: June 14th, two years prior.

Except the console threw an error code he had never seen before: ERR_WAVEFUNCTION_COLLAPSE.

Julian frowned, tapping the terminal display. He pulled up the underlying quantum logs to see why the temporal vector had failed to register. As he read through the raw data streams, the blood drained from his face.

The mathematics were unambiguous.

The device had never manipulated the time axis ($t$). Quantum particles do not travel backward along a single timeline—doing so violates fundamental thermodynamic conservation laws. Instead, the displacement wave collapsed alternate quantum states across a Hilbert space.

He hadn’t traveled back in time to his own past. He had simply jumped sideways across the multiverse to a parallel universe where those exact stock spikes, lottery numbers, and sports victories had occurred.

In his original universe, Julian Ross had vanished from his basement lab thirty-four years ago and never returned. In every universe he jumped to, he was entering an entirely distinct reality that matched his desire.

A sudden cold sweat broke out across his neck.

If every jump was a sideways leap into an infinite web of alternate realities, then his original timeline—his true home—was just one grain of sand on an infinite beach.

The terminal on his desk chimed. A news broadcast flared to life in Spanish: Target identified. Armed forces converging on the villa of international fugitive Julian Ross.

Engine roar shook the Mediterranean sky. Black military helicopters breached the horizon, weapons pods armed.

Panic surging through his veins, Julian frantically dialed random parameters into his belt and pressed trigger after trigger.

Flash. A universe where he was emperor of a dying empire, surrounded by assassins. Flash. A universe where his wealth was built on cartel blood, and his rivals were outside his door with flame throwers. Flash. A universe where the sky was choked with ash, and the military was hunting him down for biological warfare.

Every jump landed him in a reality where his pursuit of wealth and power had generated an equal and opposite force of destruction. And with every random jump, the probability of ever finding his original baseline universe approached zero.

Standing in the center of an elegant, burning marble hall as armored soldiers smashed through the gates, Julian stared down at the blinking belt.

He was the richest man in the world. He was the most powerful man in the world.

And he was lost forever in an infinite labyrinth of his own making.
