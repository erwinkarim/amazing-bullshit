The Duplicate

techjourneyman included in Horror
 2026-08-23  494 words   3 minutes 

The thin handles of the plastic grocery bag dug painfully into my fingers, carrying nothing more than a cheap box of coffee filters. Ten minutes. I had been gone for ten minutes. Yet as I pushed open the front door, the air inside felt instantly, terrifyingly wrong. The deadbolt was unlatched. From the kitchen, the familiar sizzle of searing garlic drifted down the hallway, wrapped in the warm, careless laughter of my wife and daughter.

I rounded the corner, the floorboards silent beneath my sneakers. My breath caught in my throat.

Sitting at the head of the dining table, leaning back with a beer glass in hand, was a man wearing my favorite faded olive jacket—the one with the frayed left cuff. He turned his head toward the hallway.

It was my face. Every sharp line, the faint scar across the left eyebrow, the precise way his shoulders slumped against the wood.

He cleared his throat—a dry, double-click sound I’ve made my entire life—and set his glass down. “Sarah, honey,” he called toward the stove. “I think someone just walked in.”

“Who is it?” Sarah stepped into the dining room, holding a wooden spoon. She glanced at me, then at him. There was no terror in her eyes, no shock. Only a mild, flickering irritation.

“Hey buddy,” the duplicate said. His voice was mine, resonant and calm, laced with quiet pity. He stood up, resting a hand naturally on Sarah’s waist—a gesture so intimate and practiced my stomach churned into ice. “Are you lost? Do you need help?”

“Sarah, it’s me,” I choked out, taking an aggressive step forward. My knuckles turned white around the plastic bag. “What is this? Who is this guy?”

“Sir, please,” Sarah said, her voice tightening as she pulled back toward him. “You’re scaring Maya.”

Seven-year-old Maya peeked out from behind the kitchen island, clutching her drawing pad, staring at me like I was a jagged shadow on the wall. “Daddy,” she whispered, tugging on the duplicate’s jacket. “Who is that man?”

“It’s okay, bug,” the duplicate murmured, running his thumb over her hair—my exact morning routine with her. He stepped between them and me, tall, solid, and impossibly real. “I’ve got it.”

“Get out of my house!” I screamed, but the sheer, chilling wrongness of their unified stare froze my feet to the hardwood. They didn’t see an imposter sitting at their table. They saw a crazy stranger standing in their doorway.

“We’re going to call the police if you don’t leave,” the copy said softly, his eyes filled with a terrifying, gentle sorrow. He gently closed the distance, guiding me backward with an extended palm until my heel hit the threshold.

The door clicked shut. The heavy deadbolt engaged with a final, echoing thud.

Outside, the night air was freezing. I stood alone under the harsh yellow porch light, hands trembling violently, holding a plastic bag of coffee filters for a kitchen I would never enter again.
